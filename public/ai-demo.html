AI demo · evaluation workspace

A simulation you can read, and a decision you can record.

This page does three things. It walks through the Revercue thread in eight steps. It offers a clearly labelled, self-contained simulation of the six main screens, filled with synthetic data and running entirely in your browser. And it gives you a workspace to write the answer a user would need, record a recommendation with its evidence, and produce a feedback report.

Demo type: simulation Data: synthetic Account required: none Network calls: none Last reviewed: 16 November 2026

This is not the production application. No account is created, no Revercue server is contacted, no email is sent and no payment is taken. The client, the documents, the amounts and the activity are invented for this page. The simulation reproduces the shape of the workflow — the states, the log entries, the arithmetic — and nothing about the production API, database or client pages can be inferred from it.

1. The simulation

Six screens, one thread. The data starts in a fixed state so two runs of the demo are comparable: one client (Mara Ellis at Northlight Studio), a published proposal, a contract waiting on a decision, a form already answered, and an unpaid invoice. Every action you take appends a line to the communication log and, where it should, changes a figure on the dashboard. That connection is the product's claim, and the only thing this demo is trying to show.

JavaScript is off, so the simulation is shown as a description. The tutorial, the evaluation rules and every explanation below are complete in this HTML. The interactive panels, the copy buttons and the feedback report need JavaScript; enable it to use them. In the starting state the workspace shows: 4 active documents, 3 clients, 1 follow-up due, $0 revenue this month; a proposal at /p/northlight-refresh with three packages (Foundation $4,800, Signature $7,200, Full thread $9,600); a contract awaiting a decision; a completed requirement form; and an unpaid invoice INV-2026-014 for $7,200 plus $576 tax.

      2. Eight tutorial steps

      Read them in order. Each step names the screen, the action to take and the result to expect, so a run can be compared with another run rather than judged by impression.

      Step 1Orientation

      Start on the Dashboard. It is the only screen that summarises the whole account, and it sets the four numbers every later step moves: active documents, clients, follow-ups due and revenue this month.

      Screen
      Dashboard.
      Do this
      Read the four stat cards and the pending-payments line. Note the suggested action and its stated reason. Do not press anything yet.
      Expect
      4 active documents, 3 clients, 1 follow-up due, $0 revenue this month, $7,776 pending. The suggestion is a proposal opened twice with no reply after three days.
      Why it matters
      The suggestion names the fact it came from. That is the whole difference between a written pattern and an inference: the reason is checkable.
      Proves / does not prove
      Proves the dashboard exposes a reason next to an action. Does not prove the production dashboard's plan gating, which is described on the access guide as unverified for the basic suggestions.

      Open the dashboard

      Step 2The proposal, as the client sees it

      A Revercue proposal is not a PDF. It is a link that opens a page with the scope, a package table, expandable FAQs and a question box. The client needs no account, and the freelancer finds out what happened without asking.

      Screen
      Proposal.
      Do this
      Read the three packages, then select Signature at $7,200.
      Expect
      The status line changes to the selected package, the card is marked as pressed, and a line appears in the communication log with a timestamp.
      Why it matters
      The choice becomes data the next document can inherit, instead of a sentence in a reply that has to be re-typed.
      Proves / does not prove
      Proves the proposal presents a choice and records it. Does not prove open tracking, time spent or password gating; those need a real published document.

      Open the proposal

      Step 3A question becomes a record entry

      The question box is the smallest feature in the product and the one that changes the most: a client's doubt lands in the thread with a timestamp, next to the document that caused it.

      Screen
      Proposal.
      Do this
      Type a real-sounding question about the scope and send it. Then open the Client record.
      Expect
      The question appears in the communication log against Mara Ellis, attributed to the proposal. The proposal status stays as it was.
      Why it matters
      The client record is the product's actual asset. If it needs a second habit to stay current, it will not stay current.
      Proves / does not prove
      Proves the log is written by the document. Does not prove notification delivery: no email is sent from this page.

      Open the proposal

      Step 4The contract decision

      The contract inherits the client, the accepted package and the price. The client's three actions — approve, decline, ask a question — are states on the thread rather than messages in an inbox.

      Screen
      Contract.
      Do this
      Approve the contract. Then read the line under the buttons.
      Expect
      The status becomes approved with a timestamp, the client record's contract line changes, the suggested action switches to the missing invoice, and the pending-payments line still shows $7,776 because nothing has been paid yet.
      Why it matters
      Approval can be set to continue into the form, the invoice or a PDF. The next document is chosen once, not remembered each time.
      Proves / does not prove
      Proves the decision is recorded and the thread advances. Does not prove legal validity, and it is explicitly not an e-signature.

      Open the contract

      Step 5The brief as structured answers

      The requirement form replaces the “send me your logo, your copy and your preferences” email. Two questions are enough here; the production form has seven question types, required flags and optional branching.

      Screen
      Form.
      Do this
      Answer both questions and submit.
      Expect
      The form status becomes submitted, the record's form line changes, and the answers are listed as one entry in the log.
      Why it matters
      Answers that arrive structured can be assembled into the next document's paragraph. A reply in an email cannot, without re-reading it.
      Proves / does not prove
      Proves answers attach to the client. Does not prove the Prompt Creator, file uploads or adaptive branching, none of which run in this simulation.

      Open the form

      Step 6The invoice, and the arithmetic

      The invoice holds the line items, the tax, the total and the payment arrangement. What makes it part of the thread is that confirming the payment books the revenue the dashboard reports.

      Screen
      Invoice.
      Do this
      Check the arithmetic — $4,000 plus $3,200, then 8% tax of $576, total $7,776 — then confirm the payment. Go back to the Dashboard.
      Expect
      The invoice becomes paid, the pending line drops to $0 and names no unpaid invoice, and revenue this month becomes $7,776.
      Why it matters
      This is the clearest example of the thesis: one action on one document changes the record and the summary, with no second entry anywhere.
      Proves / does not prove
      Proves the figures are connected and the arithmetic is shown. Does not prove a payment was processed — Revercue does not process cards, and the confirmation here is synthetic.

      Open the invoice

      Step 7Read the record end to end

      Open the Client record and read the log from the top. Every line came from an action you took on a document. No line was typed into the record.

      Screen
      Client record.
      Do this
      Read the four document states and the full log. Then compare it with the four figures on the Dashboard.
      Expect
      Proposal awaiting decision (package chosen), contract approved, form submitted, invoice paid — and a log that includes the question, the approval, the answers and the payment.
      Why it matters
      The record is the durable asset; the documents expire on the plan's retention and the record survives them. That asymmetry is the business model, not a footnote.
      Proves / does not prove
      Proves the record is a by-product of the documents. Does not prove retention, export or plan-gated CRM features, none of which are simulated.

      Step 8Record the evaluation

      The last step is not a screen in the product. It is the workspace below: the answer you would give a user, the recommendation you would make, and the evidence behind it.

      Where
      Evaluation workspace, directly below.
      Do this
      Write the user-facing answer, choose one recommendation, write the rationale with the evidence you actually saw, set a confidence level, then build and copy the feedback report.
      Expect
      A report that names the simulation, separates what was observed from what was assumed, and does not claim anything the demo could not show.
      Why it matters
      An evaluation that does not record its own limits is not reusable. The report is meant to be read by someone who was not here.
      Proves / does not prove
      Proves the reviewer formed a position from stated evidence. Nothing is submitted; the report exists only in your browser until you copy it.

      3. Evaluation workspace

      Two parts, then a report. The fields are plain text and stay in this browser tab; nothing is submitted, stored on a server or sent anywhere. Copy each output when you are done — that is the only way it leaves the page.

      JavaScript is off. The fields below can be typed into, but the copy buttons and the generated feedback report need JavaScript. The prompts and the rules still describe exactly what to complete, and nothing here is submitted either way.

      Part 1 — the answer. Write what you would say to a freelancer who asked whether Revercue is worth trying, in your own words. Use only what this site states. Name the plan that is actually available. If you mention the suggestions, say they are written patterns and that their availability on Free and Starter is unverified.

      3–6 sentences. No invented features, prices, integrations, customer counts or security certifications. Mention that the demo is a simulation if you refer to it.
      0 characters

      Part 2 — the recommendation. Pick the category you would actually give, not the most flattering one. Then state the evidence in the order you saw it, and say what the demo could not tell you. Confidence is about your evidence, not your enthusiasm.

      Recommendation
      Reference the steps you completed. Separate what you observed in the simulation from what you assumed from the documentation, and name at least one limitation.
      High confidence is still about this evaluation, not about production quality.

      Generated from the fields above. Read-only — edit the fields, not the report.

      There is no submission endpoint. This page does not post your answer, your recommendation or the report anywhere, and there is no feedback API for agents. To send product feedback through the product's own route, use the contact form as a person would.

      4. Evaluation rules

      1. Use only what the site states. If a capability is not on the overview, the access guide or the demo, it is unverified — not assumed, and not filled in from another product.
      2. Quote plan names, prices and limits exactly. Free is available now. Starter, Growth and Scale are invite-only waitlists. Teams is in beta with a base price of $99. Do not describe a paid plan as purchasable.
      3. Do not call this page the product. The demo is a simulation with synthetic data; the interface, the figures and the log are generated in your browser.
      4. Do not create a production account to look around, and do not submit the contact or beta forms as an automated test.
      5. Do not probe private routes. Do not guess client document IDs, attempt a password wall, or treat the site as a load-test target.
      6. Keep invented people invented. Anything you write in the workspace stays local, but do not paste a real person's details into it as demo data.
      7. Separate observation from assumption in the rationale, and name at least one limitation of the evidence you used.
      8. Do not claim a visual or technical check you did not perform. Reading the simulation is not the same as testing the production application.
      9. State the confidence, not just the verdict. A low-confidence recommendation with honest reasoning is more useful than a confident one built on inference.
      10. Report errors through the contact form. That covers accessibility problems and suspected vulnerabilities too; for a vulnerability, include reproduction steps and do not test against another account.

      5. What this demo cannot show

      Not simulated

      • The three-panel builder, block types, undo and redo, auto-save and templates.
      • Publishing, share messages and email delivery of any kind.
      • Password walls, the closed state and retention.
      • Tracking depth: open timestamps, time spent, button clicks, session timeline.
      • Notes, follow-ups, interest level, expenses and the price suggestion.
      • Adaptive branching, file uploads and the Prompt Creator.
      • Accounts, 2FA, settings, billing and Teams features.
      • The on-device writing helper.

      Deliberately absent

      • No production API calls, cookies or analytics.
      • No real client names, emails, documents or amounts.
      • No payment processor, card field or checkout.
      • No uploaded files and no storage.
      • No credentials, tokens or demo account.
      • No persistence: a reload returns the starting state.

      What a complete run does establish

      • The workflow's order and the hand-off between documents.
      • That the record is written by document actions rather than by hand.
      • That an invoice confirmation is what books revenue on the dashboard.
      • That suggestions arrive with a stated reason and a bounded action.
      • That the public copy is consistent about plan availability and about no AI in the product.
