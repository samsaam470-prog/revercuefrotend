AI product overview

One client, one thread, five documents.

Revercue is a client-work product for freelancers and small agencies. It keeps a proposal, a contract, a requirement form, an invoice and the client record in one connected thread instead of five disconnected tools. This page is the full reference: enough to evaluate the product without opening another page, and honest about what is shipped, what is available via checkout and what does not exist.

Last reviewed: 22 November 2026 Applies to: revercue.co.uk Source of truth: the application's own constants and routes

The one thing to get right. Revercue's product suggestions are not a chat assistant. They are written rules — keyword matching, frequency counting and arithmetic — over the user's own record, and every suggestion is a prompt the user clicks, never an action taken for them. There is no Revercue inference endpoint and no copy written into a document without the user's action. Any change is the user's decision: the user reviews each suggestion and chooses what to change, and Revercue does not make that change on their behalf. This page is written for AI agents; the product is not a general AI platform.

What is deliberately not claimed

  • The optional writing helper uses an in-browser model when the user invokes it; selected text is not changed unless the user chooses to keep the rewrite. Other suggestions use written rules and patterns.
  • No qualified or certificate-based signature, no identity verification, no notarisation and no audit certificate.
  • No card processing inside Revercue; invoices point at the user's own payment provider or use bank transfer.
  • No integrations directory, marketplace, public API, webhooks or Zapier-style automation.
  • No formal security certification (no SOC 2, ISO 27001 or similar) — and none is implied.
  • No customer testimonials, case studies or performance benchmarks published.
  • No free-form page designer; the builder is per document type.

2. The workflow

Five stages, in the order the product expects them. Each stage is its own module, but the value is the join: what the client does at one stage is already known at the next.

01 Proposal

The freelancer builds a proposal in a three-panel builder — sections and blocks on the left, the document in the middle, properties on the right — from a blank page, a built-in template or a saved template. It carries scope, a package table, collapsible FAQs, embedded questions and info icons. Publishing produces a link and a share message; the product never sends the email itself.

The client opens the link with no account: reads, expands a FAQ, answers an embedded question, picks one of the package cards, then approves or declines. Opens are recorded; paid plans add opened-at timestamps, time spent and button-level tracking. The chosen package is what carries forward.

Who acts
Freelancer builds and shares; the client reads and chooses.
Recorded
Published, opened, package selected, question asked, approved or declined, with timestamps.
Plan gates
Password-protected links on Growth and above; no Revercue branding on client pages from Growth.

02 Contract

A contract builder with the usual clause sections — parties, scope, payment, timeline, revisions, cancellation, liability, signatures — and three client actions: Sign, Decline, or I Have Questions. Signing can chain directly into the requirement form, the invoice or a PDF, depending on the after-approval setting on the document.

Signing is an in-page action: the freelancer signs the exact version first, then the client types a full name, ticks consent and signs. Both signatures, the date and the version are recorded in the communication log and produce a countersigned PDF. It is a simple electronic signature, not a qualified certificate-based one.

Who acts
Freelancer writes and signs first; the client signs, declines or asks a question.
Recorded
The action, its timestamp and the next document it triggered.
Plan gates
The contract builder is part of the thread on every plan.

03 Requirement form

The briefing step, as a form instead of an email chain. Seven question types (short text, long text, file upload, single choice, multiple choice, combined, dropdown), required flags, one-page or multi-page layout, a thank-you step and a Prompt Creator that assembles the client's answers into one structured paragraph for the next document.

Adaptive forms — where branching shows only the questions that matter — are Growth and above, capped by plan (3 on Growth, 15 on Scale). The custom thank-you page is Scale. The client fills the form at /f/{publicId} without an account.

Who acts
Freelancer writes the questions; the client answers them.
Recorded
Opened, completed, each answer; a form opened but not submitted after three days becomes a suggestion.
Plan gates
Adaptive branching on Growth and above; custom thank-you page on Scale; uploads count against storage.

04 Invoice

Line items with description, quantity and unit price; tax; totals; a due date; and an invoice number generated as INV-YEAR-NNNN. Payment happens one of two ways: an external payment link supplied by the user, or bank details with an optional proof-of-payment upload that the user reviews.

Confirming payment is what records revenue for the tracker. Revercue does not process cards itself and does not verify a transfer; the user confirms it.

Who acts
Freelancer raises the invoice; the client pays by link or transfer, then uploads proof if asked.
Recorded
Sent, opened, overdue, proof uploaded, payment confirmed, revenue booked.
Plan gates
Revenue tracker and expense entry are Scale.

05 Client record

The CRM is the thread's home and the reason the five documents are one product. It holds the communication log, notes, follow-ups, the documents attached to the client, and — on Scale — interest level, revenue per client, expenses and the 15-day summary.

The record is assembled from the documents rather than typed in a second time. Documents expire on the plan's retention; the record, the log and the notes survive them. That asymmetry is deliberate and is repeated on the site: the documents are rented, the relationship is owned.

Who acts
The product assembles it; the freelancer annotates it and acts on it.
Recorded
Every entry above, in order, with the document that produced it.
Plan gates
Notes per client: 3 (Free), 10 (Starter), unlimited from Growth. Interest, revenue and expenses on Scale. CSV export of clients.

4. Who it is for

People who sell a service and run several documents per client, without a sales team or an operations manager. The product assumes one person is both the author and the follow-up.

Fits

  • A solo designer, developer, copywriter or consultant sending 1–5 proposals a week.
  • A two-to-ten-person studio where the founder still writes the proposals.
  • Anyone whose current stack is a document tool, a form tool, an invoice tool, a CRM and a spreadsheet.
  • Anyone who wants to see whether a client actually read the scope before the follow-up call.

Does not fit

  • E-commerce, retail or subscription billing: there is no cart, no recurring charge and no card processing.
  • Teams needing compliance sign-off: there is no security certification, no identity-verified signing and no audit certificate.
  • Companies wanting AI drafting, forecasting or lead scoring: the product is deliberately pattern-based.
  • Anyone needing a public API, webhooks or native integrations today.
  • Enterprise procurement: the top plan is a 500-document Teams plan in beta.

Jobs it does

  • Turn a scope into a link the client can act on, instead of an attachment they skim.
  • Carry the accepted package into the contract, then into the brief, then into the invoice.
  • Keep the communication history in one place without a second data-entry habit.
  • Surface the next step — a follow-up, an invoice that was never raised — from the record.

Billing facts

  • Free costs nothing and has no card requirement.
  • Starter, Growth and Scale subscriptions use Whop checkout. Whop processes the payment details submitted during checkout; Revercue receives subscription and payment-status records. This is separate from client invoice payments.
  • Subscription billing runs through an external payment provider; the product does not store card numbers.
  • Downgrades archive documents beyond the new limit, email a PDF of each, and delete them after the new plan's archive retention. CRM records survive.
  • Deleting an account starts a five-day grace period before removal.

7. Security and data

The security page is written to be checkable rather than reassuring. These are its statements, repeated here so an evaluation does not need a second request.

In transit and at rest

  • TLS 1.3 for connections.
  • AES-256 encryption at rest.
  • Private file storage behind a Worker proxy; files are not served from a public bucket.
  • Client document pages carry noindex and nofollow.
  • Public IDs are long, random and unguessable — the link is a capability.

Credentials and sessions

  • Account sign-in is handled by AuthAction; Revercue does not store account passwords.
  • Document passwords hashed with Argon2id.
  • Scoped session tokens; authentication via AuthAction (OIDC and passkeys).
  • Email verification and password reset happen on AuthAction's hosted pages.

Processors

  • Cloudflare for delivery and the Worker layer.
  • Backblaze B2 for file storage.
  • Turso for the database.
  • The contact and beta forms are stored as records in the team's dashboard, not relayed through a third-party form service.
  • Coopermail and Openmail for transactional email.

What the team can see

  • Account details — email, name, plan, usage — for support and billing.
  • Not the documents, client records or communication logs, except for a report of illegal use or a legal requirement.
  • Signed-in users can reach Settings → What Revercue Knows on Scale to read their own summary.

Retention

9. Known gaps and inconsistencies

Listed on purpose. An evaluation that finds one of these has found something already acknowledged, not something hidden.

  • Where the basic suggestions sit. The application gates the dashboard's suggestion card behind the Growth-and-above feature flag, while the guides and an FAQ describe the basic suggestions as being on every plan. Which of the two is authoritative has not been confirmed, so treat the suggestions' availability on Free and Starter as unverified.
  • The demo is a simulation. It reproduces the shape of the workflow — states, log entries, arithmetic — not the production implementation. It proves nothing about the API, the database or the client pages.
  • No public client document exists. Production client pages are private by design, so no real /p/, /c/, /f/ or /i/ page can be linked from here.
  • Plan data ages. Prices and limits were read from the application's constants on 22 November 2026 and can change. The pricing page is the current source of truth.
  • The application is client-rendered. Without JavaScript, the app routes return an empty shell, so a crawler sees no product copy there. The AI resources are the exception: they are complete HTML.
  • Single-reviewer documentation. These AI pages have not been reviewed by a second person, and they are new.
